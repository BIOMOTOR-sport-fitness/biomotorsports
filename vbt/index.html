How to use
  1. Pick the athlete in Athletes, then add exercises, sets, load and target velocity in Workout (or start from a template).
  2. Place the phone / iPad on a tripod at plate height, side-on (side view), 2–3 m away. The whole plate must be visible.
  3. Start the Live camera. Two indicators sit at the top right: 📱 Stable (phone upright and still) and ◎ Plate. The yellow ring is the plate the app will track. As soon as the bar moves, the largest moving plate locks automatically. You can also tap the plate centre and edge first and Lock it – then live feedback starts from the first rep.
  4. Tap “Tap to record”, do the set, then tap again to Stop. If the plate was found in the middle of the set, the app re-analyses the recording (“Processing…”). Then Set review opens: warnings, Save / Discard and Next (next set's weight).
  5. A single set without a workout: ⚡ Quick. An old video: Load video → Analyse video – the plate is found automatically.
  6. Left toolbar: plate size, feedback (Read / Target / Loss · M Vel / P Vel / ROM / Power), eccentric tempo, camera flip, zoom, recording on/off.

  2. Start the camera and aim at the bar from the side.

  4. Tap Tap to record, do the set, tap again to stop. The set review opens: Save, Discard or Next.

Inside a workout

  1. Workout → add exercises and sets with weights and target velocity (or start from a template).
  2. Tap 📷 on a set row to record that set.
  3. After Stop the review opens; Next shows the next set's weight.
  4. Set row ⋯ menu: Import video (analyse an existing clip) or Mark complete (no video).

Place the camera

The app tracks the plate, not the bar. Keep that plate in clear view for the whole set.

  • Round plates on the camera side (bumper or iron). Enter the real plate diameter in the ◎ toolbar button – it is the ruler for every measurement.
  • Plate in frame for the whole set – a rep is lost when the plate leaves the frame.
  • A still phone: any phone movement reads as bar movement. Use a tripod, clamp or stable shelf.
  • Even light: keep bright windows out of the frame; dark plates in a dark room are hard to see.
  1. Angle: film from the side, within 0–30° of the end of the bar.
  2. Height: bar height at the middle of the lift. Floor for deadlifts; between knee and chest for bench press and squat; chest to shoulder for cleans and snatches.
  3. Distance: far enough to keep bar and plates in frame through the whole rep (2–3 m). Fine-tune with zoom.
  4. Orientation: portrait.

Framing causes more bad sets than anything else – fit the whole movement with room to spare. Next most common: filming hand-held.

Logging a workout

  • Start: Workout → Start an empty workout, start a program, or Repeat a past workout (History ⋯).
  • Add exercises: search the library, tick several at once, Create new exercise if it is missing. Barbell exercises (📷) record bar speed with the camera; dumbbell, cable, machine and bodyweight work (✍) is logged by hand – load, reps, seconds, RPE and ✓.
  • Supersets: exercise ⋯ → Superset with above / below. They get labels A1/A2 and the camera alternates their sets.
  • Exercise ⋯: move up/down, Swap exercise (keeps sets and weights, before a set is done), log by hand / track with camera, Delete exercise & sets. Set ⋯: Import a video, Mark complete, Delete set.
  • Barbell tracking Off → the workout becomes a simple logger.
  • Rest timers: the header counts from the last completed set of any exercise; inside each exercise from its last set. On a tracked set rest counts from the last detected rep, not from Stop.
  • Finish workout: summary with duration, sets, tonnage, today's PRs and which lifts trended faster or slower → Save workout only, Save & create program, or Save & update program (when it came from a program and changed). Programs are shared with every athlete on this device's roster.
  • Marked-complete sets save load and reps but stay out of velocity trends and e1RM. Deleting a set deletes its video and cannot be undone.

Building programs

A program (template) is a saved plan: exercises and their order, supersets, each set's planned kg, reps, seconds / target speed and RPE, exercise notes and coaching notes. Sets you record from it go to History – never into the program.

  • Create: Workout → ＋ Create a program; or Finish workout → Save & create program; or History ⋯ → Save as program.
  • Type to add: name sets x reps @ kg – rdl 3x8@60, back squat 3x5 at 100, bench press 4 × 6 @ 82,5. Use x or ×, @ or at, point or comma. ↑↓ choose a match, Enter adds, Tab completes the name, Esc clears. db, bb, kb = dumbbell, barbell, kettlebell. Up to 20 sets. An exercise already in the list is highlighted instead of added twice. No match → Create custom. Press A or / to jump to the field. The same field works inside a live workout.
  • Edit sets: Add set, ✕ a set, ＋ Exercise note (e.g. “Rest 3 min”), ＋ Coaching notes under the name, Apply zone for the target speed.
  • Reorder & supersets: drag ⠿ to the top or bottom of another card to move it; drop onto the middle to superset. On a phone use ⋯ → Move up/down / Superset with above/below.
  • The builder saves as you type – the header shows Draft, Saving… or Saved. Click Done.
  • Share: Share with entire team, or tick athletes. The start screen shows each athlete only the programs shared with them.
  • Programs ⋯: Start, Edit, Duplicate, Share, Archive / Unarchive, Delete. Edits apply the next time a workout starts – a workout in progress and history do not change. Deleting keeps past workouts.
  • Name programs so they stay clear: by day (“Monday: Squat”), focus (“Speed Squat”) or block (“Block 1: Hypertrophy”).

Sharing programs with your team

  • Program ⋯ → Share with team / Manage sharing: switch on Share with entire team, or tick athletes. The program appears on each chosen athlete's Workout start screen.
  • Start team workout…: start one program for several athletes at once, then record each athlete by switching the athlete chip (top right).
  • Send as link: shares or copies a link holding the whole program. Opening it (or pasting it in Programs → 🔗 Add from link) on another phone adds the program there – handy until cloud sync arrives.
  • Programs keep their supersets. Put instructions for athletes in an exercise note.
  • What athletes can change: loads, reps, sets, add / swap / skip exercises during a workout. Those changes stay in that one workout; the program does not change unless the coach chooses Save & update program.
  • Editing a shared program updates it for everyone at once; a workout already in progress keeps the version it started with.
  • Stop sharing: the program leaves those athletes' start screens. Workouts they already completed stay in their History.
  • In the builder, ✕ (Cancel) on a new program discards it – tap Done to keep it. Closing an existing program keeps your changes (it saves as you type).
  • Effort target: plan each set with RPE or RIR (toggle in the builder).

Manual exercise entry

  • Dumbbell, cable, machine, bodyweight (✍): type load and reps (or seconds / metres), tap the box ✓ at the end of the row. The next set is highlighted, ready below it. The ＋ at the top of the table adds or removes a field: Weight, Reps, Duration, Distance.
  • Barbell set without recording (warm-up, failed recording): set ⋯ → Mark complete. The same menu has Record set and Import a video.
  • Whole workout without camera: ⚙ Training preferences at the bottom → Enable barbell tracking Off. The Exertion metric there (Off / RPE / RIR) picks the effort column.
  • A manual set keeps load, reps (or time / distance), RPE or RIR, its place in the workout and history, and counts for your 🔥 weekly streak (every week with a completed workout).
  • Weaker e1RM? e1RM and velocity trends use tracked reps only – record your heaviest set instead of marking it complete (e1RM needs 3 tracked loads).
  • Forgot a set? Add it before Finish workout – or open the workout from History (⋯ → Open) to add, edit or delete its sets.

Custom exercises

  • Search the library (or type in the exercise field), find nothing, tap Create new exercise / Create custom. It is saved to your library for every workout and program.
  • Exercise name must be unique – otherwise you see “Exercise name already in use” and it does not save.
  • Type: Barbell, Bodyweight, Cable, Dumbbell, Kettlebell, Machine, Time, Distance or Other. The type sets the fields: Time = duration + reps, Distance = distance + duration, Bodyweight = reps, the rest = load + reps.
  • Track velocity (Barbell only): on = the camera records bar speed, power, ROM and bar path for every rep (needs a round plate on the camera side); off, or any other type = logged by hand.
  • Exercise ⋯ → Exercise settings: 1RM estimation on/off, minimum (1RM) velocity, default metric / training focus.
  • Name it well – the name is what you see in history, charts and exports: the variation (Pause Squat 2 s, Tempo Bench 4-1-1-0, SSB Squat) or the setup (Pin Squat pin 4, Block Pull 10 cm). Make a separate exercise for each specialty bar – each has its own load–velocity profile.
  • Wrong name or type? Delete it (🗑 in the library – cannot be undone; past sets keep the name) and create it again.

RPE and RIR

  • Turn on a rating: Workout → ⚙ Training preferences → Exertion metric: RPE (6–10), RIR (reps in reserve) or None (default – never asks). On adds an exertion column to hand-logged set rows; off removes it.
  • Rate a set: tap Stop – the Enter an RPE (or RIR) sheet opens while the video processes. Tap a rating or Skip (use Skip on warm-ups). On a hand-logged set, pick it in the exertion column.
  • Scale: 6, 6.5 … 10 plus Under 5.5 (warm-up). RPE→RIR: 10/9.5 = 0, 9/8.5 = 1, 8/7.5 = 2, 7/6.5 = 3, 6 = 4, warm-up = 5+ (reps-in-reserve RPE scale, Zourdos et al. 2016; the lower number of a range). One value is stored per set – switch scales any time.
  • Where it shows: set rows (workout and history), the set review, the CSV export, and the exercise card's RPE e1RM + RPE table – estimated from the heaviest set rated RPE 6 or higher with 10 reps or fewer (reps + RIR, Epley). The table gives loads for 1–10 reps at @10/@9/@8/@7. Skipped sets stay blank and are not used.
  • Read RPE and bar speed together: a higher RPE usually means a slower bar. The review flags High rating, fast bar (rating too high, or a good day) and Low rating, slow bar (technique problem or a poor day). Watch the video before you trust either one.

Estimated 1RM

Example: squat 40, 60, 70 and 80 kg in one workout and track each set – the app draws your load–velocity line and follows it down to your minimum velocity (bar speed of a true max). The load there is today's e1RM – no max attempt.

  • Needs, for one exercise in one workout: 3 different loads (3 sets at one weight = one load); a wide spread – heaviest ≥60% above lightest, or bar ≥0.25 m/s faster at the lightest; tracked reps (camera or imported video); Track velocity on. Until then e1RM shows –.– (the card says what is missing) – add a set at a new weight.
  • Where: exercise card → Show profile (e1RM above the load–velocity chart), the small % 1RM under each set's kg (today's e1RM, or your latest one), Progress, the PDF report and Athletes → Team leaderboard → Best e1RM. Each exercise has its own e1RM (squat ≠ front squat; high-bar ≠ low-bar).
  • Exercise settings (⋯): 1RM estimation on/off, minimum velocity (only when on), mean or peak velocity.
  • Set loads: in Show profile type a % – e.g. 82% of 150 kg = 123 kg – and Apply to open sets. Work it out again from each day's e1RM.
  • With RPE on you also get a separate RPE e1RM (heaviest set rated RPE 6+, ≤10 reps).
  • Trust it less with a narrow spread or a long extrapolation (add a set near your heaviest working load), scattered points from a bad rep (check the set review), and for the deadlift (max bar speed varies between days – Ruf et al., 2018). Test a true 1RM before a competition or after a long break.

Load–velocity & power profiles

  • Work up through a few loads on one barbell exercise, then on the exercise card tap Show profile. ‹ › arrows move between the Velocity profile, the Power profile and the RPE table. Hide profile closes it.
  • One exercise in one workout = one profile; every workout gives a fresh one. Quick sets are saved into the day's “Quick sets” workout.
  • How it is built: the fastest rep at each load (mean velocity by default) = one point per load; a straight line through them. The line appears with 2 loads; faint dots show every rep. e1RM needs 3 loads and a wide spread.
  • Spread your loads: 60–70 kg gives a short line one odd rep can tilt; 40–100 kg gives a long, stable one. Record your warm-ups – each adds a lighter load.
  • Compare: a grey line behind today's – choose 6-week avg or Last time in ⚙ Training preferences → Context timeframe. Today's line below it = slower than usual; above = faster.
  • Power profile: best power at each load with a fitted curve; the dashed Max power line marks the highest value; the load under the top of the curve is where you made the most power. Mean or peak: exercise ⋯ → Exercise settings → Default metric.
  • Programming: velocity-targeted load (type a speed – e.g. 0.60 m/s – and read the load), power blocks (prescribe near the top of the power curve), read the day (compare today's warm-up line with the 6-week avg before heavy sets).
  • Profiles work on barbell exercises with Track velocity on, custom ones too. Each exercise and each bar (axle, SSB, log) has its own profile; a new stance, grip or style changes the line. Coaches: switch athlete and open a workout from History to see that athlete's profiles.

Switch List / Grid; search by exercise, or by athlete name with “All athletes”. Tap a video to play it with the velocity overlay, download, share, export with overlay, open the set, or delete.

Coach Mode

  • Athletes tab → Coach: add your name and switch Coach Mode on to record a roster of athletes from your own phone. You pick who is recording, and every set lands in that athlete's history. Switch it off to record your own training (as “your name (self)”).
  • Pick who records: the athlete roster, or – in a team workout – the athlete pictures at the top of the Workout and Track screens (tap ＋ to add the next athlete).
  • Start a team workout (Workout start screen, or program ⋯): pick an empty workout or a program and tick the athletes. Each athlete keeps their own workout on this device.
  • Team views: Team leaderboard on the Athletes tab.
  • Wrong athlete? History ⋯ → Change athlete → name moves the whole workout.
  • Export data: Report → choose the range and This athlete / All athletes / one athlete → Export data (CSV). The JSON backup moves the whole roster to another device.
  • The Coach Mode switch will not move? Finish or close the open workout first.
  • Athletes recording on their own phones, synced to your roster, needs the cloud backend (next step). Until then send programs as links and merge data with the backup file.

Managing your athlete roster

  • Add one athlete: Athletes → Add an athlete → first name, last name, email (sport and body mass optional). Tick “Send an invite” if they will also record on their own phone. You can record for them on your device straight away.
  • Invite (athlete ⋯ Edit → 📨 Send invite): shares a personal link (share sheet or email). On their phone the athlete opens it in Chrome / Safari and taps Accept – they get their profile and every program shared with them, recording as themselves. The roster shows “✉ invite sent”. Until cloud sync arrives, they send their training back with Report → Backup, and you merge it with Restore.
  • Bulk import: three spreadsheet columns – first name, last name, email – copy, paste under 📋 Bulk import, choose “Send invite emails?”, Parse & Preview, then Import N athletes. The preview flags missing fields, bad emails and emails that appear twice, and skips athletes already on your roster.
  • Seats: Coach → Roster seats. When the roster is full, Add an athlete is greyed out and a bulk import that needs more seats stops – import fewer rows or add seats.
  • Assistant coaches: list them under Coach → Roster seats & assistant coaches and send them the backup file to share the roster. Coaches do not use a seat.
  • Remove an athlete: Edit → Remove from roster (tap twice). It unlinks them from your team views but keeps their workouts and videos – restore them any time from “Removed athletes”. Delete athlete erases everything.
  • Invite to the wrong email? Check the address with the athlete before you send it.

Editing athlete profiles

  • Athletes → tap Edit on an athlete (or 👤 My training profile for yourself) → change the fields → Save. The most recent edit wins.
  • Fields: first and last name (roster, leaderboard, exports, PDF), profile picture (roster, leaderboard, team pictures, header), gender (leaderboard filter), date of birth (age), height, sport / squad (leaderboard squad filter) and bodyweight.
  • Keep bodyweight current: weigh athletes and update it before the next session. Relative strength (e1RM ÷ BW) and peak power per kg use the bodyweight saved with each workout when it was recorded – a new bodyweight changes scores from then on, not sets already recorded. The profile keeps a weigh-in log.
  • No relative score? The bodyweight was blank when the set was recorded – add it now and later sets get one (the leaderboard lists athletes without bodyweight). Scores look off? Weigh the athlete and update it.

Multi-athlete workouts

  • One phone on a tripod, several athletes rotating through it – each keeps their own workout, sets and history. Three to six athletes per phone works well.
  • Start: Coach Mode on → Workout → 👥 Start a team workout → choose an empty workout or a program and tick the first athletes.
  • Add the next athlete: tap ＋ in the athlete pictures at the top → pick the athlete → same exercises, empty, or a program.
  • Switch: tap the next athlete's picture – the workout (and Track screen) changes to theirs. Record their set, then switch again.
  • Finish: when an athlete finishes and saves, the workout goes to their history and they leave the team workout; the screen moves to the next athlete. The others stay open until they finish too.
  • An athlete with a workout open on their own phone and one on yours keeps both – each saves as a separate workout. The Coach Mode switch moves only after every team workout is finished.

Team leaderboard

  • Athletes tab → Team leaderboard: pick the Exercise and the Score; it ranks the roster and shows the top 10 – picture, name, exercise, score and how many days ago they last trained it.
  • Scores: Estimated 1RM (needs 3 loads with a wide spread), Relative 1RM, Weight (heaviest load lifted), Relative weight (e.g. 2.1× BW), Mean power (highest single-rep mean power), Relative mean power (W/kg), peak power and relative peak power, best mean velocity, latest readiness.
  • Narrow it with Gender (All / Male / Female – “Other” shows under All), Squad and Time frame (3 weeks to 1 year, or all time).
  • Absolute or relative: relative scores divide by the bodyweight saved with the workout – on a roster with very different bodyweights the two lists look very different. Keep bodyweights current.
  • Athlete visibility (Coach card): athletes using the app with Coach Mode off still see the leaderboard.

Video storage and sharing

  • ⚙ Video settings (Videos tab): Save videos in-app (off = kept 24 h), Auto-export to camera roll, Keep videos for 30 / 60 / 90 / 180 days or no limit, and Keep favourites forever. Mark a set ★ Favourite in its review; “★ only” filters the library. Retention deletes old videos for good – the set's bar speed, reps and history stay.

  • Cloud video storage (upload with each set, watch on any device, coach's switch covering the roster, storage pool) needs the Biomotor cloud backend – it is the next big step. Until then videos stay on the phone that recorded them.

Warm-ups, hidden reps, second metric

  • Auto warm-up: barbell exercise ⋯ → Add warm-up sets… → type today's working weight; warm-ups at about 40, 60, 75 and 85% (5, 3, 2, 1 reps) go before your first set – record them and the load–velocity profile builds itself.

Training metrics

Pick each exercise's training focus in the Workout tab – it sets the on-screen metric and the set-review highlights. If you watch only two numbers, make them mean velocity and mean power.

FocusPrimarySecondary
Heavy strengthMean velocity, e1RMRPE, velocity loss
Speed & powerPeak velocity, peak powerTime to peak velocity
HypertrophyVelocity lossRPE, mean velocity
Olympic liftingPeak velocity, peak powerBar path, rep duration
Athletes (S&C)Mean & peak velocity, eccentric powerPower, e1RM
Tempo & techniqueRep duration, phase splitsROM, bar path

Per rep

  • Mean velocity – average bar speed over the concentric; default for most lifts, above all heavy strength.
  • Peak velocity – fastest instant of the concentric; for jumps, speed work and Olympic lifts (varies more rep to rep).
  • Time to peak velocity – shorter = force produced faster.
  • Mean power = mean velocity × load × g. Peak power = load × g × peak velocity (estimate). Eccentric power – how hard the bar is braked on the way down: high = stopping a fast-falling bar (drop catch), low = controlled lowering.
  • ROM – vertical travel of the plate in the concentric (not hips or body).
  • Tempo: eccentric, bottom pause, concentric, top pause; rep duration is their sum.
  • Entered by you: load (needed for power and e1RM), RPE / RIR in the set review.

Per set, exercise and across sessions

  • Set: total reps, averages, best, velocity loss (best → last rep). Stopping sets at 20 % loss gave the same strength gain as 40 % with 40 % fewer reps and more jump improvement; 40 % gave more quadriceps growth (Pareja-Blanco et al., 2017).
  • Exercise: e1RM from the session's load–velocity profile (needs 3 different loads), max power, tonnage, time under tension.
  • Across sessions: trend / readiness vs same-weight sets of the last 6 weeks, personal records (load, reps, velocities, powers, e1RM, relative strength), and the team leaderboard in Athletes.
  • Export: Report → Export data – every metric is a column; choose this workout, 7 days, 30 days or everything.

Real-time feedback & tempo beep

Track → 📣 Feedback → switch on → pick a Mode and a Metric.

  • Read – speaks every rep's score as soon as the rep ends. Metrics: mean / peak velocity, ROM, mean / peak power, time to peak velocity.
  • Target – a bell for a rep at or above the target, a buzzer below it. Type the target or drag the slider. e.g. 1000 W on cleans; 0.40 m/s on strength sets in a taper – stop at the first buzzer.
  • Loss – velocity loss vs the fastest rep of the set (mean or peak velocity). Alert 0–60 %, default 20. Silent while the set is fresh; once a rep passes the alert by more than 2 points the loss is read out every rep – end the set or do one last rep.
  • ⏱ Tempo beep – a beep every second from the moment you start recording. Pace a slow eccentric or a paused rep; works alone or with any mode. The review shows the tempo actually lifted: eccentric, bottom pause, concentric and top pause.

When to use it

  • Working sets: Target and Loss hold intent and stop you at the right effort without looking at the screen.
  • Picking a working weight: every rep rings the bell → add load; buzzer from rep 1 → take some off.
  • Technique work, warm-ups, max singles & doubles: leave feedback off.
  • Busy gym: pair Bluetooth earbuds – cues play at media volume.

When it goes wrong

  • Spoken and final values differ – each rep is measured live, then again after the set; trust the set review.
  • A skipped cue – if a cue is still playing when the next rep ends, that cue is skipped. The review still shows every rep.
  • A silent rep – if the plate is lost during a rep, that rep stays silent. Keep the plate in frame.

Common recording issues

Missing reps or odd numbers? Start with what the camera could see – the app tracks the plate on the camera side.

What the app does with a bad recording

  • Drops reps when the plate leaves the frame.
  • Flags the set with a warning in the review (wrong angle, too dark, low contrast, no reps…).
  • Keeps what it measured – check 📱 Stable before recording: a shaking phone reads as bar movement.

Lighting (💡 indicator)

  • Too dark: the camera raises its gain and the picture turns grainy. Turn on lights, change the angle, or move the bar into better light. Dark plates + dark clothes + dark room is the hardest setup.
  • Bright light behind the bar: a window or lamp turns the bar into a dark outline. Film from the other side, or move the light into a corner of the frame.

Keep the phone still

Never hand-held: tripod, phone stand, or rest it against something solid (e.g. a kettlebell on a box).

Angle and position

  • Front or back: the plate cannot be tracked – move to the side (0–30° of the bar end). A slightly oval plate (up to 30°) is corrected automatically.
  • Too high or too low: keep the phone within the bar's travel height.
  • Too close / too far: the ◎ indicator turns green when the plate size in frame is right.

The plate

  • Non-round plates (12-sided, hex, square): swap in a round bumper or iron plate on the camera side.
  • Small or hidden plate: keep a large round plate in clear view (not behind a collar).
  • Wrong plate size: every speed is off – fix with Recalculate in the review.
  • Low contrast: a plain background in a different colour helps.

Still wrong?

  1. Run the Device check below.
  2. Go through “Recording a set” again.
  3. In the set review tap 🐞 Report a problem and send the file plus the video to your Biomotor admin.

Device check

Importing a video

Film on any camera, import the file, and the app tracks it like a live recording – same set review, same metrics. Live recording also uses the phone's motion sensor, so record live when you can and import when you cannot.

Film the video

  • Frame rate: 60 fps. The import screen warns below 55 fps; the review shows Slow frame rate below 50 fps.
  • Phone position: still, side-on to the bar (same rules as live).
  • Resolution: 1080p and 4K both work; 4K takes longer.
  • Camera: phone, action camera or mirrorless – H.264 plays everywhere; H.265/HEVC only in Safari (iPhone: Camera → Formats → Most Compatible).

Three ways to import

  • Without a workout: Track → Load video → “Save as: Without a workout” → exercise, weight, reps → Analyse video.
  • Inside a workout: Workout → set row ⋯ → Import a video.
  • A whole workout from video: import the first set, then in the review tap Start a workout and upload more and import the rest.

Set the plate size on the import screen before analysing. Imported clips go into the video library with their filmed date, with share, download and delete. Wrong plate size? Fix it in the review with Recalculate – no need to import again.

Custom plate sizes

Distances are worked out from the plate's diameter, so a wrong size gives wrong bar speeds. Default: standard 45 cm Olympic / bumper plate.

When to change it

  • A change plate is the largest on the bar – enter its size (much smaller than 45 cm).
  • A rubber-coated or tri-grip plate – diameter varies by brand, measure it.
  • A 1-inch gym plate – these come in many sizes.
  • A tracking disc – for an exercise without plates, fit a round disc and enter its diameter.

Only the largest plate counts: two 20 kg bumpers with a 5 kg change plate outside them → set the bumper size. Measure across the full face with a tape; round to the nearest centimetre.

Set it before you record

  1. Track → tap ◎ (plate size) – the button shows the current diameter.
  2. Switch Use default off.
  3. Enter the diameter in cm (more than 10 cm) or tap a preset.

The custom size stays on for every set (the ◎ button turns amber) until you switch the default back on. For an imported video, set it before tapping Analyse video. Wrong size after the set? Unlike most apps you can fix it in the set review – enter the real diameter and tap Recalculate.

Plate preview

While you frame the shot the ring may jump between large and small plates – that does not matter. Once the bar moves, the app locks onto the largest moving plate.

🔬 Accuracy check

Measure how closely Biomotor agrees with a reference device (GymAware, Vitruve, Enode, a linear transducer…) on the very same reps. These are your own numbers – quote them together with the conditions they came from.

How to run a fair test
  1. Attach the reference device (tether to the bar, or its sensor) and film the same set with Biomotor at the same time.
  2. Start with the best set-up: phone side-on to the plate, steady, 1–2 m away, 60 fps, good light. That is your baseline.
  3. Cover the loads you train with – light, medium and heavy (e.g. 45, 60, 75% 1RM) – and the lifts you use (squat, bench, deadlift, cleans).
  4. Then repeat with one change at a time (45° angle, 30 fps, dim light, 3–5 m) and tag each set, so the breakdown shows which condition costs accuracy.
  5. Type or paste the reference device's mean and peak velocity per rep. Skip reps the device missed – leave the box empty.
  6. Aim for 50+ paired reps from more than one lifter before quoting numbers. CCC or ICC above 0.90 = excellent agreement.
